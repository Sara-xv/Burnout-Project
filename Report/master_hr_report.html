Missing Values — Real Issue, Resolved

10 numeric columns had missing values ranging from 0.77% to 1.68% per column (2,922 of 25,000 rows affected, 0.48% of all cells). The pattern was checked and found to be random (MCAR) — not concentrated in any particular role, work mode, or burnout risk group. Resolved via median imputation.

Invalid Values — False Positive (No Action Needed)

An automated profiling tool flagged 16,382 values in presenteeism_days_per_month and absenteeism_days_per_month as "invalid" using a rule that expected values between 1 and 12 (as if these were calendar months). These columns actually represent a count of days per month, not a month number — the rule was misapplied due to the _per_month suffix in the column name. No correction needed.

Outliers — Mostly Expected

16 of 21 numeric columns had statistical outliers flagged by IQR methods, but their contribution to the overall quality penalty was minor. Columns like overtime_hours_per_week (13.3% flagged) are naturally right-skewed — some employees genuinely work far more overtime than others. Not treated as defects.

11. Final Summary — What Drives Burnout Here

🔴 Strongest Risk Drivers

Stress level, workload index, and hours worked per week show the strongest positive correlation with burnout risk. High-risk burnout jumps from 2.3% at 30-39 hrs/week to 57.7% at 60+ hrs/week.

🟢 Strongest Protective Factors

Work-life balance and sleep quality show the strongest negative correlation — higher scores on these consistently mean lower burnout risk.

🔴 Worst Combination Found

Low manager support + high workload index together produce an 87.5% high-risk rate — far worse than either factor alone would suggest.

🟢 Manager Support is a Real Buffer

Even under high workload, employees with high manager support have a meaningfully lower high-risk rate (70.7%) than those with low support (87.5%) — especially for Manager and Executive roles.

🔴 Role Sensitivity Differs

At 60+ hrs/week, Managers reach 73.8% high-risk while Support staff stay at 29.4% — the same hours affect different roles very differently.

⚪ No Major Non-Linear Surprises

Mutual information analysis confirms the top non-linear drivers match the top linear drivers — the relationships in this dataset are mostly straightforward and monotonic.
